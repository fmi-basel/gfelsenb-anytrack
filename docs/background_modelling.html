The problem — ghost flies in the local background

Tracking subtracts each frame against a per-ROI background (one image per arena, at tracking resolution) and looks for dark blobs. That background must be the clean arena floor with the fly removed. A fly that keeps moving is a statistical minority at every pixel, so a simple per-pixel high percentile already recovers the floor. But where a fly dwells for a long stretch, its dark silhouette dominates that pixel's samples and bakes into the background — a ghost fly. Subtraction there goes to ≈0 (the real fly becomes invisible and the tracker loses it), or, with a brighter-biased estimator, a too-bright patch appears and produces a phantom candidate whenever the fly steps off. Both failure modes are what "no ghost flies on any background" is asking us to eliminate.

This is strictly the local background. The full-frame model background (used only for Hough ROI detection and the QC overlay) is not modelled here — it does not downscale and its consumers are indifferent to a dwell ghost.

Methods benchmarked

  • GMM — per-pixel 1-or-2-component Gaussian mixture, keep the brighter component mean. The legacy default. Robust in principle, but its small-sample bright estimate at a dwell spot overshoots the floor → a bright patch → phantom candidates.
  • Percentile p90 — per-pixel 90th percentile. Cheapest; clean as long as no fly dwells past the 90th percentile of samples.
  • fg-excluded — percentile computed only from frames where a provisional track puts the fly far from that pixel. Cleanest in theory, but needs a track and is dominated by a slow nanpercentile.
  • de-ghost (post-pass, applied on top of any of the above) — detects a baked-in dwell pixel (near-max margin brighter than the background, inside the arena disk) and refills it from a Gaussian mixture of nearby clean-floor pixels (unbiased; the earlier near-max fill overshot). Fixture-safe: a genuinely static dark object is dark at the near-max too, so it is never lifted.
  • adaptive — model_background, described below.

The adaptive model — model_background

The insight from the benchmark: the expensive methods rarely earn their cost. Most arenas are clean and a single percentile suffices; only dwell spots need work; and one case is genuinely impossible. So the function measures difficulty and pays only for what it needs:

T0 — fast. Per-pixel percentile p90 (config bg_model_percentile). If no arena pixel is baked in (near-max not margin brighter than the baseline), return it. Easy arenas pay one np.percentile.
T1 — de-ghost. If any pixel is baked in, run the track-free neighbor-fill de-ghost. Recovers the true floor at a dwell spot from surrounding clean pixels. This clears every recoverable ghost in the dataset.
T2 — fg-excluded. Only if T1 still leaves a ghost and a provisional track is available: recompute the percentile with the tracked fly masked out, then de-ghost again. Most work; essentially never needed in this dataset.
Impossible — never-moved fly. A fly that never moves is dark in ~every frame, so no within-background statistic can tell it from arena structure (a fixture, or the naturally-dim periphery). We verified this: a darkness proxy (fraction of the arena still dark after de-ghost) is 35–49% for every arena and does not separate the stuck arenas (35–46%) from the clean ones — see below. So model_background does not flag it; it is caught by its tracking outcome instead (collapsed detection coverage, and the QC edge-pinned / low-activity flags).

Attempted — can we recover a never-moved fly instead of flagging it?

Whether a baked-in fly can be salvaged rather than flagged, revisited after adding odor-port detection (which reliably locates the central port — see odor_port.py):

  • GMM. No — a fly dark in ~every frame is a one-component (dark) GMM at its pixel; there is no second (floor) mode to prefer, so the fly is the mode and bakes in.
  • Naive spatial fill (fill every compact local dark spot). Fails: the only compact dark spot is the central port (~276 px in every arena), so it fills the port → a false candidate every frame (multi-candidate → ~100%), and the earlier "coverage 6%→100%" was an artifact (counting the filled port, not the fly).
  • Fill the fly, spare the port (refine_stuck_background, opt-in two-pass --refine-stuck). With the port now detectable (excluded from the fill) and a coverage gate (only run where a fly is actually stuck), a fly mask — from Segment-Anything's segment_fly, or the dark connected component when the fly is compact — is diffused in from the surrounding floor. Concept-validated with an oracle fly mask: coverage 6–17% → 100% with 95–98% of detections at the fly (not the port). So a baked-in stationary fly is recoverable in principle — my earlier "impossible spatially" verdict was too absolute.

And on 2025-12-11 it works. A faithful streaming diagnostic (the ROI decoded through the same FFmpeg crop→scale→gray filter as production, so it matches the production background) confirms the flagged arena_01 is genuinely under-detected: only ~0.04 candidates/frame, 4% coverage — the fly is baked in and invisible to subtraction. Running the two-pass --refine-stuck (fill the fly, spare the port) takes it to 100% coverage with a tight track (std ≈4 px), end-to-end 54%→100%. (An earlier reading of this arena as a "linking" problem came from a confounded probe — cv2-decoded frames subtracted against the FFmpeg-built background reintroduce the rim scaler mismatch and manufacture spurious candidates; the faithful decode shows the real cause is under-detection.)

Conclusion: a baked-in stationary fly is recoverable (fill-fly-spare-port, oracle- validated) and shipped as opt-in --refine-stuck; a perfectly never-moved fly with no baked-in background signature is still best handled by flagging via its tracking outcome. Port detection is the enabling piece for both — and a feature in its own right (fly-to-port distance, the local-search readout).

How to optimize background modelling — recommendations

  1. Default to bg_model="adaptive". It is percentile-fast on easy arenas and only escalates where a fly dwells — best speed/accuracy on this dataset.
  2. Drop GMM as the local default. Its brighter-component estimate overshoots at dwell spots and drives the multi-candidate rate up (55% vs 0.0% for adaptive).
  3. Don't pay for fg-excluded. ~26× slower for no cleanliness gain over neighbor-fill de-ghost here; keep it only as the T2 fallback.
  4. Use neighbor fill, not near-max (bg_deghost_fill="neighbor"): unbiased floor recovery avoids the brighter-patch phantom.
  5. Treat stationary suspects as QC, not a modelling target. A truly never-moved fly is information-theoretically indistinguishable from a fixture in a single clip; flag it.

Config knobs

  • bg_model — "adaptive" | "gmm" (local background method)
  • bg_model_percentile — T0 baseline percentile (default 90)
  • bg_deghost_margin — gray-levels a pixel must be darker than its near-max to count as a ghost (≈ detector threshold)
  • bg_deghost_fill — "neighbor" | "nearmax"
  • bg_deghost_percentile — the near-max percentile used to detect a baked-in dwell pixel (default 98)
