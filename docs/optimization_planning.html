anytrack — Performance Optimization Planning

Where batch tracking time actually goes, what has been optimized, the hard ceilings we hit (and why), and a stage-by-stage catalogue of ideas worth trying next. Numbers are from an Apple M3 Pro (5 performance + 6 efficiency cores) on real mpeg4 clips (2176×2176, 4 arenas, ~18 000 frames).

Last updated: 2026-07-04 · companion to pipeline.html & streaming_bg_parity.html

1 · Where the time goes

4 · Why the ceilings exist

Decode is the floor, and it can't be cheaply skipped

The source is mpeg4 with GOP-12: one keyframe (I) followed by 11 predicted (P) frames, repeating. Each P-frame is a delta from the previous frame, so reconstructing frame N requires decoding every frame back to the keyframe.

  • "Decode only every 2nd frame" is impossible for inter-coded video — the skipped frames are required inputs, not independent images. FFmpeg's select filter reflects this: it decodes everything and only drops afterward (no decode saving).
  • -skip_frame nokey can skip P-frame decode, but only delivers keyframes — every 12th frame here (~1.7 fps), far too sparse for tracking.
  • Truly independent per-Nth decode needs an all-intra codec (MJPEG/FFV1/rawvideo) where every frame is a keyframe.
  • roi_downscale scales after decode, so it shrinks the tracking pixels but not the full-res decode — which is why downscale=4 gave almost no speedup.

Only 5 performance cores

The M3 Pro has 5 performance + 6 efficiency cores. A single streaming video already uses ~4 tracking workers + FFmpeg on the fast cores; a second concurrent video spills onto the slow E-cores — so K=2 concurrency adds only ~1.22× (measured on local disk, so it isn't a network artifact).

Bottom line: 8–10× is not reachable on mpeg4 + this hardware. The two doors to real gains are a decode-friendly codec and more performance cores. Everything in §5 is framed around that.

5 · Future optimization ideas, by stage

Ranked roughly by impact ÷ effort within each stage. Tags: expected impact and effort.

Decode & I/O — the current bottleneck

highest-impact area
  • Try -lowres for mpeg4 high low effort — FFmpeg's mpeg4/mpeg2/h263 decoders support -lowres 1/2, which decodes directly at ½/¼ resolution by scaling the IDCT — cheaper than full-decode-then-scale. This is the one untried lever that could actually cut decode cost on the current codec, and it pairs naturally with downscaled tracking. Measure decode time + tracking accuracy at -lowres 1.
  • Re-encode sources to H.264/HEVC high med effort — unlocks the already-built VideoToolbox hardware-decode path (a no-op on mpeg4). One-time re-encode cost amortizes if clips are analyzed more than once. Best if combined with acquisition-side changes.
  • Acquisition-side codec high policy — record in H.264 (HW-decodable) or an all-intra codec (per-Nth decode becomes possible), and at the resolution actually needed. 2176² may be more than required for centroid tracking.
  • Staging + prefetch for network sources med med effort — stage_video_locally="auto" copies to local NVMe; add a background prefetch so video N+1 stages while N tracks (overlap network I/O with compute). Matters when a batch reads many cold clips over the network.
  • Keyframe-only preview mode niche low effort — -skip_frame nokey for a fast ~every-12th scrub (QC preview, coarse activity check), not full tracking.

ROI detection & background — big easy batch win

easy batch win
  • Detect ROIs + background once per session, reuse across the batch high low effort — arenas are fixed in a rig, so a day's clips share geometry. Skipping per-video ROI detection saves ~10 s × (N−1) videos. Add a --rois-from <video> / cached-ROI path; validate the background still matches (illumination drift → refresh).
  • Fewer background samples small low effort — gmm_n_samples (100) drives both time and memory; 50–60 may suffice for a static arena.
  • Cap BLAS threads during concurrent GMM fits small low effort — with K videos, concurrent numpy EM fits oversubscribe BLAS; set OMP/OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS per worker (as we did for OpenCV).

Concurrency & scheduling

  • Pin tracking workers to performance cores med med effort — on macOS set worker thread/process QoS to USER_INITIATED so the scheduler prefers P-cores; on Linux use sched_setaffinity. Could make K=2 land on real cores instead of E-cores.
  • Persistent worker pool across videos med med effort — under spawn, each video re-spawns 4 workers + a Manager; a pool reused across the batch amortizes that. Matters most for many short clips.
  • Stage-pipelined scheduler med high effort — instead of K independent full pipelines, explicitly overlap video A's decode with video B's tracking (producer/consumer across stages) to fill cores more evenly than coarse per-video threads.

Detection hot loop

already ~889 fps/ROI — low priority
  • Reuse pre-allocated cv2 output buffers (dst=) for subtract/threshold/morph to cut per-frame allocations.
  • Collapse the two morphology passes (open 3×3 + close 5×5) to one where the mask is clean enough.
  • Skip centroid_contrast when QC diagnostics aren't requested.
  • OpenCL/UMat or Metal for diff+threshold+morph — likely not worth it at current speed unless resolution grows.

Pose stage (when enabled)

  • Larger MPS batches across ROIs & videos high — the crop-batch inference is throughput-bound on batch size; feed the GPU wider batches (already benchmarked device×batch×every_n).
  • Overlap pose with tracking med — run keypoint inference on completed crops while later frames still track, instead of a serial post-pass.
  • pose_every_n + interpolation (already available) — the same speed/accuracy trade as frame stride, but for keypoints.

Hardware

  • More performance cores high — M-series Max/Ultra or a many-core workstation lifts the concurrency ceiling directly (K scales with fast cores).
  • GPU decode high — NVIDIA NVDEC (Linux, h264/hevc) or VideoToolbox (macOS, h264/hevc) offloads decode entirely; needs a HW-decodable codec (ties back to §5 decode).
