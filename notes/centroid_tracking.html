The Centroid Tracker

How anytrack turns a pile of per-frame blob detections into one continuous fly trajectory per arena — the logic in tracker.py, explained intuitively.

← Pipeline overview Background modelling Streaming parity Tutorial
The one-sentence version

Every arena holds exactly one fly. Each frame the detector hands the tracker a handful of candidate blobs; the tracker's whole job is to answer “which of these is my fly?” — frame after frame, without ever losing the thread — and to keep going gracefully through frames where the fly briefly vanishes.

  • In: up to 5 candidate blobs per frame (centroid + fitted ellipse + area).
  • Out: one smoothed (x, y) per frame, or “no fly this frame”.
  • How: predict → gate → pick nearest → update, with a miss-counter that widens the search and re-acquires when the fly reappears.
  • Assumption that makes it simple: one object per arena, so there is no multi-target assignment, no track births/deaths — just one track to keep alive.

1 · The mental model

Imagine standing over an arena in the dark with a flashlight. You know roughly where the fly should be next, so you point the beam there. If a fly-shaped blob falls inside the beam, that's your fly — you note its position and swing the beam to follow. If the beam catches nothing (the fly sat still and faded into the background, or ducked behind a fixture), you don't panic: you hold the beam where you last saw it and widen it a little each frame. Only if the fly stays gone too long do you give up on continuity and grab the biggest thing moving in the arena to start again.

That flashlight is the tracker. The beam's aim is the prediction, its radius is the acceptance gate (max_jump), and “too long” is miss_tolerance. Everything below is that idea made precise.

2 · What comes in — the candidates

The tracker never looks at pixels. Upstream, detector.py runs the same recipe on every frame and hands the tracker a short list of candidates:

a
Background subtraction (directional)
cv2.subtract(background, frame) — bgdiff_type="dark" keeps only pixels darker than the background, because a fly is a dark object on a bright arena floor.
↓
b
Threshold + morphology
Binarize at thr_fixed = 15 gray levels, then open(3) to drop salt-speckle and close(5) to fill the fly into one solid blob.
↓
c
Contours → ellipse → area filter
Each blob's contour is fit with cv2.fitEllipse; blobs outside [10, 1500] px² are discarded. What survives becomes an EllipseCandidate: x, y, angle, major, minor, area.
↓
d
Keep the top 5 by area
Sorted largest-first, the top detect_max_candidates = 5 are surfaced. Not just the biggest — handing the tracker several lets it pick the fly by position, so a static artifact of similar size can't hijack the track.
The tracker is coordinate-system agnostic. In the fast path these candidates live in scaled-local pixels (the downscaled ROI crop). The tracker works entirely in that frame; the caller converts the final answer back to full-frame coordinates afterward.

3 · The per-frame decision

Given this frame's candidates, CentroidTracker.step() runs the same five moves:

1
Predict where the fly should be
While locked (0 recent misses) the Kalman filter extrapolates from position + velocity — a moving fly's next spot. While lost the aim snaps back to the last confirmed position instead (see §6 for why this matters).
↓
2
Set the gate radius
eff_jump = max_jump · (1 + misses). Fresh off a good frame the gate is just max_jump; each missed frame widens it, so a fast dart or a re-appearance farther away is caught within a frame or two.
↓
3
Greedy nearest-neighbour assign
Of all candidates, take the one closest to the predicted point — but only if it falls inside the gate. With one object per arena there's nothing to disambiguate against: nearest-inside wins.
↓
4
Hit → update & smooth
Reset the miss counter, remember the position, and feed the measurement to the Kalman filter, which returns a jitter-smoothed (x, y). That's the frame's output.
↓
5
Miss → count, wait, and eventually re-acquire
No candidate in the gate → increment misses and emit nothing for this frame. Stay this way for up to miss_tolerance = 15 frames. Beyond that, give up on continuity and re-seed on the largest candidate in the arena (or the arena centre if truly empty).

4 · The acceptance gate, visually

The gate is a circle of radius max_jump centred on the prediction. Among candidates inside it, the nearest is chosen; everything outside is ignored — which is exactly how a static artifact or a stray speckle gets filtered out. Each missed frame inflates the radius (amber) so the fly is re-caught quickly.

5 · Three states, one track

The miss counter puts the tracker in one of three regimes. There is never more than one track (id 1); these are its moods, not separate objects.

A hit always returns to LOCKED. Misses walk right through RECOVERING; if the fly comes back the filter is re-seeded on it (not nudged), and once the streak passes miss_tolerance the tracker abandons continuity and RE-ACQUIRES on the largest blob — the arena's one real object.

6 · Design choices that took real bugs to find

The core loop is textbook; the interesting parts are the corrections layered on top. Each fixed a concrete mistracking on the localsearch clips.

Re-acquire on the strongest blob, not the arena centre

The original re-init after a long loss snapped back to the ROI centre. A fly that spent the clip near the wall was then permanently out of reach of the gate — it was never re-found. Seeding on the largest candidate instead (the single object actually in the arena) took arena_01 from 3% → 98.9% tracked and arena_03 from 84.7% → 99.7%, with centred flies unchanged.

While lost, aim at the last position — not the Kalman extrapolation

A constant-velocity filter keeps coasting in the fly's last heading. But flies usually stop when you lose them (they landed, or sat still and faded into the background). Coasting marches the gate away from where the fly actually is, so it keeps rejecting the real detection for all 15 frames. Anchoring the search at the last confirmed position during a miss streak fixes that.

On recovery, re-seed the filter — don't just correct it

When the fly reappears after a gap, the Kalman filter's velocity is stale garbage. Feeding one correction leaves a bad velocity that flings the next prediction off and re-loses the fly. So a recovering hit throws the filter away and starts a fresh one at the found position. Together with the two fixes above, a fast excursion now re-acquires in ~1–3 frames instead of waiting out ~15 (arena_01 98.9% → 99.6%, arena_03 99.7% → 100%).

7 · The Kalman filter's actual job

It's easy to overstate the Kalman filter's role. It is not the thing that decides which blob is the fly — that's the gate + nearest-neighbour rule. The filter (Kalman2D, a constant-velocity model with state [x, y, vx, vy]) does two humbler things:

  • Predict — give the gate a smarter centre than “last position” when the fly is moving steadily.
  • Smooth — average out per-frame centroid jitter so the trajectory isn't noisy. Its noise terms (process 1e-2, measurement 1e-1) keep it trusting the measurement closely while shaving the wobble.

Turn it off (use_kalman=False) and tracking still works — you just get the raw centroid each frame and the gate is anchored at the last position throughout.

So a “no fly this frame” output is not a hole in the final data. Misses within tolerance and stride-skips are reindexed and interpolated downstream, so the parquet carries a value for every frame; the tracker's job is only to place the confident anchors.
